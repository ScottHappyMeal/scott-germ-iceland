02 / The week

Seven dates, four usable hours of sun, zero interest in November.

You land at dawn on Dec 29, so the hotel stay is five nights, Dec 29 to Jan 3, unless you add the optional Dec 28 hold.

Dec 28, Monday

Denver lets you go at 3:40 PM

Icelandair FI670, a 737 MAX, overnight to Keflavik. You leave in the afternoon and skip an entire night of sleep on purpose.

Delta's JFK, MSP, and DTW flights to KEF are summer-only. Late December is Icelandair weather: a year-round DEN nonstop, about 7 hours, which is why this flight is already in the plan.

Photo: Wikimedia Commons, Icelandair 737 MAX.

Dec 29, Tuesday

A 6:05 AM landing, then the harbor

Check in at The Reykjavik EDITION, wander Hafnartorg and Laugavegur, and take the evening to Grotta. Sky Lagoon is the optional warm ending, 15 minutes from the hotel.

The sun shows up around 11:20 AM, so the morning is streetlights and coffee. Sidewalks can be ice rinks in nicer clothes.

EDITION on Google Maps

Dec 30, Wednesday

Optional glacier overnight, if the weather plays nice

Fly Reykjavik domestic (RKV, not KEF) to Hofn in about 55 minutes. Walk a blue ice cave, watch Diamond Beach around the late 11 AM sunrise, look for seals, and hope the lights show up over the icebergs.

Winter cancellations happen. The safer buffer is a Dec 29 to 30 hop, then back for New Year's Eve. Check flight status, and check road.is plus safetravel.is before you drive.
Dec 31, Thursday

Back in town for bonfires, a comedy hour, and fireworks

If you flew east, come back for New Year's Eve in Reykjavik. Neighborhood bonfires first, then Aramotaskaup on TV, then midnight fireworks from Hallgrimskirkja, the Perlan hillside, or the EDITION's harbor door.

It is a late, cold, crowded hour. Dress like you plan to stand still in the wind, because you will.

Jan 1, Friday

Horses first, Golden Circle if anything is open

Ishestar's Horses and Waffles ride in Hafnarfjordur: 50 minutes through lava fields, overalls and studded shoes included, waffle after. Then an optional Golden Circle if roads and energy allow.

Jan 1, a lot of Reykjavik sleeps in and stays closed. The horses do not take bank holidays as seriously as the cafes. Eat before you leave, or pack a snack that is not your pride.
Jan 2, Saturday

South Coast drama, or a glass box in a field

Seljalandsfoss, Skogafoss, and Reynisfjara near Vik, or a night in a glass lodge or bubble if you want the sky as a ceiling. Kolaportid flea market is the in-town backup, usually a weekend thing. Confirm holiday hours.

Before any South Coast drive, check road.is and safetravel.is. The waterfalls do not care about your itinerary. Stay well up the beach at Reynisfjara. Sneaker waves are not a metaphor.
Jan 3, Sunday

One last soak, then a 4:55 PM flight home

Blue Lagoon late morning, 20 minutes from the airport, then Keflavik at 4:55 PM and Denver at 6:00 PM. The water stays about 98 to 104 F year-round. Put conditioner in, keep your hair dry, and check eruption status the day before.

05 / The car

A 4x4, a Gold card, and a firm grip on the door.

Pickup and dropoff at Keflavik, after the 6:05 AM landing on Dec 29 and before the 4:55 PM flight on Jan 3. That is a 6-day rental, not a padded 7-day week. Vehicle prices below are a live Blue Car Rental quote for those exact times, converted at 123.4 ISK per USD. Hertz Iceland is the featured desk, inside arrivals, because you already have Gold Plus Rewards.

Featured desk

Hertz Iceland, the local franchise at KEF

This is Bílaleiga Flugleiða on hertz.is, not a generic Hertz.com counter in Ohio. The desk sits in the arrivals hall, no shuttle, winter hours 5:30 AM to 1:00 AM, which means a 6:05 AM landing is legally civilized.

Bring the Gold card, leave the member number off this page, and let the desk do the recognizing.

  • Winter fleet on this desk includes a Dacia Duster 4x4, a Kia Sportage automatic 4x4, a Toyota Land Cruiser 250, a Land Rover Defender 110, and a guaranteed Range Rover Sport PHEV (age 25, two credit cards, no F-roads). Hertz lists the Defender as a 5-door 110, or similar, age 23+, two cards. A 3-door Defender 90 did not show up on Hertz or on the other desks we checked. F-roads are closed in winter anyway.
  • Winter tires are standard from Nov 1 to April 15 (verified). Studded tires are allowed and recommended for ice, but at Hertz they are an option on request, not automatic. Ask at the desk.
  • Included in the rate: CDW and third-party liability, plus VAT and unlimited mileage. A US license is fine if it is the original physical card, in English or Roman script, held for at least a year.
  • Add the cover that actually matters here: gravel, sand and ash, theft, glass, and zero excess. Hertz MAX Cover is 5,950 ISK ($48) a day on a 4x4, or 4,950 ISK ($40) on a small car, and drops the excess to zero for covered damage. Medium Cover is 3,850 ISK ($31) a day on a 4x4, with a 65,000 ISK ($527) excess. Underbody, river crossings, and off-road driving are never covered. Roadside help is 990 ISK ($8) a day with MAX, or 1,990 ISK ($16) alone.
  • They do not take a cash deposit. They place a hold on a physical card in the main driver's name. Luxury and the Land Cruiser 250 want two cards. Airport service charge is 5,500 ISK ($45) per rental. Extra driver is 1,150 ISK ($9) a day, capped at 8,050 ISK ($65), unless the desk waives it.
  • Gold Plus Rewards points cannot be earned in Iceland right now (verified on hertz.is). Ask for Icelandair Saga Club points instead. The Gold page still lists a 5% rate discount, expedited pickup, and a free additional driver for a spouse or partner. The local price list still shows the extra-driver fee, so treat those perks as "check at the counter."
  • This is a KEF-to-KEF loop, so skip one-way fees. A live Hertz quote for these exact dates was not locked here; their published winter ranges sit in the same neighborhood as the Blue numbers below. Book on hertz.is and add your Hertz number there, not on this page.

Three ways to get around the ice

Winter driving, the unromantic version

  • Ice and wind: hold the door when you open it, or the Atlantic will take it as a souvenir.
  • Gravel shoulders and one-lane bridges are not optional scenery. Slow down and yield like a guest.
  • F-roads are closed in winter. Your insurance agrees, loudly.
  • Speed cameras have no holiday spirit.
  • Check road.is and safetravel.is before you leave the hotel, not after the first closed pass.
  • Many gas pumps want a card with a PIN, not a hopeful tap.
  • You get a little more than four hours of daylight. Leave early. Be in before the dark does the driving.
  • Do not stop on the Ring Road for photos. The glacier is not going anywhere. The truck behind you might.

07 / The armor

Pack for ice, wind, and a swimsuit you will not regret.

Winter packing, no debates

  • Crampons or microspikes. Reykjavik sidewalks in late December are a contact sport.
  • A waterproof shell. "Water resistant" is a suggestion the Atlantic will ignore.
  • Wool base layers. Cotton is a sad souvenir.
  • A swimsuit for the lagoons, plus a cheap conditioner you can sacrifice to Blue Lagoon silica.
  • Warm gloves, a hat that stays on in wind, and boots with actual grip.

Season notes that matter

Reykjavik sunrise sits around 11:20 AM, sunset around 3:35 to 3:45 PM, a little more than four hours of daylight. Typical air is about -3 to 3 C, with wind, snow, and ice in the mix.

Northern lights love these long dark nights. Cloud cover is the real gate. Watch the cloud map on vedur.is, not just the KP number.

Dec 31 is fireworks night. Jan 1, many shops and restaurants are closed. Before the South Coast, check road.is and safetravel.is.
